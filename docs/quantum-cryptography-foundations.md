# Quantum-Cryptography Foundations

## Evidence boundary

This page is an introductory orientation, not a cryptographic standard, implementation guide, capability forecast, or proof of a quantum-computer timeline.

## Why migration matters

Widely deployed public-key systems based on integer factorization or discrete logarithms may be vulnerable to a sufficiently capable fault-tolerant quantum computer running algorithms such as Shor's. Long-lived information may also face harvest-now-decrypt-later exposure before such a computer exists.

## Current migration foundation

NIST has finalized ML-KEM, ML-DSA, and SLH-DSA. Their use still requires system-specific protocol design, implementation assurance, interoperability testing, key management, performance analysis, recovery, and applicable policy review.

## QKD and QRNG

Quantum key distribution and quantum random-number generation are context-dependent engineering options. They do not replace post-quantum public-key migration, authentication, secure update, trust governance, or recovery.

Use the [Standards Register](standards-register.md) for current publication status and applicability.
