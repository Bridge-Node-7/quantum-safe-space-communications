# Methodology

## Core rule

The framework separates exposure, readiness, evidence, and critical conditions because they answer different questions.

| Layer | Question | Orientation |
|---|---|---|
| Quantum Exposure Severity | What may be harmed, how severely, and how urgently? | Higher is worse |
| Migration Readiness Profile | What capability has been demonstrated? | Higher is stronger |
| Evidence Confidence Ledger | How well is each claim supported? | Separate, not compensating |
| Critical Risk Overrides | What condition requires action or accountable exception review? | Non-compensatory |

## Sequence

1. Define scope and exclusions.
2. Map communications links.
3. Build the cryptographic inventory.
4. Record exposure severity per item.
5. Complete the ten-domain readiness profile.
6. Link claims to evidence records.
7. identify critical overrides and owners.
8. Review QKD or QRNG only where justified.
9. Build the migration roadmap and action plan.
10. Assemble the Decision Pack.

## No weighted aggregate

The methodology does not average or weight exposure and readiness into one number. Strong governance cannot offset failed command authentication. A pilot cannot offset missing key custody. A large evidence volume cannot offset a critical unsupported claim.

## Unknowns

Unknown, stale, conflicting, unavailable, or out-of-scope information must remain visible. Absence of evidence is not evidence of low exposure or high readiness.

## Human governance

The framework structures evidence and decisions. It does not automate authorization. Accountable human authorities retain responsibility for scope, exceptions, resources, deployment, and risk acceptance.
The framework does not provide certification or operational authorization.
