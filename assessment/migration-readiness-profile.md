# Migration Readiness Profile

## Orientation

**Higher stages mean stronger demonstrated migration capability.**

This is a profile across ten domains. It is not a weighted index, average, percentile, or certification score. Strong performance in one domain does not erase a weak, unknown, or critical condition elsewhere.

## Common stage rubric

| Stage | Label | Demonstration standard |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | Not Assessed | Scope or evidence is insufficient to characterize the domain |
| 1 | Initiated | Ownership or initial activity exists, but the domain is incomplete or mostly self-attested |
| 2 | Defined | Documented process, scope, requirements, and accountable ownership exist |
| 3 | Piloted | The process or control has been exercised in a representative environment with evidence |
| 4 | Deployed | The capability is implemented across the approved scope with governed operations |
| 5 | Sustained and Reassessed | The capability is monitored, tested, recovered, reviewed, and updated through an established cycle |

## Ten readiness domains

### Domain 1. Governance and accountability

Decision rights, owners, risk acceptance, funding, policy, and escalation are established.

### Domain 2. Cryptographic inventory and visibility

Assets, links, protocols, implementations, keys, certificates, trust anchors, and vendors are identified and maintained.

### Domain 3. Exposure characterization

Data lifetime, cryptographic vulnerability, mission consequence, urgency, and unknowns are documented.

### Domain 4. Critical-link protection

Command, telemetry, navigation, timing, update, recovery, and other critical trust paths are prioritized and protected.

### Domain 5. Migration strategy and sequencing

A bounded roadmap identifies transition patterns, dependencies, milestones, exit criteria, rollback, and decommissioning.

### Domain 6. Crypto-agility architecture

Systems can change algorithms, parameters, protocols, keys, and trust anchors without uncontrolled redesign or downgrade.

### Domain 7. Implementation and interoperability

Candidate controls are integrated, tested, measured, and shown to interoperate within mission constraints.

### Domain 8. Key and trust management

Key generation, custody, distribution, rotation, revocation, recovery, and trust-anchor governance are controlled.

### Domain 9. Vendor and supply-chain readiness

Vendor claims, product versions, dependencies, support dates, provenance, evidence, and migration commitments are managed.

### Domain 10. Monitoring, recovery, and reassessment

The organization monitors standards, vulnerabilities, drift, control performance, recovery readiness, and reassessment triggers.
